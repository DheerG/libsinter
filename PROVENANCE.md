# Provenance of Sinter's format tools

A format tool is anything the decoder must run to reproduce the output: syntax, entropy coding, prediction, transforms, filters and reconstruction. Each one must either come from a publication dated before 2004, implemented as published with small listed changes, or be new to Sinter, with a record of the search for the same idea in patents and papers. Nothing comes from H.264/AVC, its drafts, or any later standard or codec (VP8 onward, Daala, AV1).

Encoder-only methods (motion search, mode decisions, rate control, quantizer choices) don't affect the format and aren't listed.

This file records where each tool comes from. It is not a patent clearance.

## Frame and block structure

| Tool | Source | Date | Changes from the source |
|------|--------|------|-------------------------|
| I-, P- and B-frames, frame reordering | ISO/IEC 11172-2 (MPEG-1 Video) | 1993 | As published: B-frames predict forward from the older anchor, backward from the newer, or from the rounded average of both; nothing predicts from a B-frame; frames are sent in decode order and each frame header carries its display number. GOPs are closed (no B-frame predicts across a key frame). |
| Frame header | ITU-T H.263 picture layer: fixed-length fields, with the picture size sent once and later pictures sending only what changes; ISO/IEC 11172-2 (MPEG-1) temporal reference for display order | 1996, 1993 | Key frames send type, QP, width, height, chroma QP offset and a 32-bit frame number; other frames send type, QP and their frame number relative to the key frame as a signed exponential Golomb number. Then a chroma flag, the global motion vectors, and the length of the escape section. The range-coded bytes run to the end of the frame, whose size the container gives. |
| Macroblock and block modes | ITU-T H.263 (macroblock types: not coded, INTER with one vector, INTRA) and Annex F (INTER4V: four 8x8 vectors) | 1996 | A 16x16 macroblock in a P- or B-frame is skipped, coded with one vector (or one per direction in B-frames), or split into four 8x8 blocks in H.263's block order. In a split macroblock each 8x8 block chooses intra, inter or skip on its own; H.263 makes the intra/inter choice per macroblock. Key frames and partial macroblocks at the frame edge are coded as 8x8 blocks with no macroblock mode. |
| Skipped blocks | ISO/IEC 11172-2 (MPEG-1 Video): a skipped macroblock in a B-picture repeats the previous macroblock's prediction type and vectors; ISO/IEC 14496-2 (MPEG-4 Part 2) version 2: a not-coded macroblock in a GMC picture is predicted with the global motion; ITU-T H.263 (COD = 1) | 1993, 2000, 1996 | Applies to 8x8 blocks and 16x16 macroblocks, in P- and B-frames (MPEG-1 applies its rule in B-pictures only). A skipped block repeats the direction and vectors of the block to its left when that block is inter-predicted, as MPEG-1 does; otherwise it is predicted with the frame's global motion (below), bi-directionally in B-frames. A skipped macroblock takes the rule of its top-left block. Nothing else is coded, as with H.263's COD = 1. The block keeps that motion for its neighbours' vector prediction. Chroma follows the co-located luma block. |
| Global motion compensation | ISO/IEC 14496-2 (MPEG-4 Part 2) version 2, global motion compensation with one warping point (translation) | 2000 | One global vector per reference and frame, in quarter-sample units, coded as signed exponential Golomb numbers in the frame header (MPEG-4 codes warping-point trajectories). It is used by skipped blocks only (MPEG-4 also lets coded macroblocks choose it). B-frames have one per direction (MPEG-4 uses GMC in S-VOPs only). Prediction uses the B-spline interpolation. |

## Prediction

| Tool | Source | Date | Changes from the source |
|------|--------|------|-------------------------|
| Quarter-sample motion vectors | ISO/IEC 14496-2 (MPEG-4 Part 2) quarter-sample motion compensation | 2000 | Vectors are in quarter-sample units, as published; the interpolation is the B-spline below, not MPEG-4's filter. |
| Luma interpolation | M. Unser, A. Aldroubi and M. Eden, "Fast B-spline transforms for continuous image representation and interpolation", IEEE Trans. PAMI 13(3); M. Unser, "B-spline signal processing, Part II", IEEE Trans. Signal Processing 41(2) | 1991, 1993 | Cubic B-spline with the published recursive prefilter (pole sqrt(3) - 2, mirror boundaries), in fixed point: constants in Q16, coefficients in Q8. The frame is first extended by repeating its edge samples 32 pixels on each side. Whole-sample positions copy the reference, which equals the exact spline there. |
| Chroma interpolation | Bilinear interpolation at 1/8-sample positions, as in the sprite and global motion warping of ISO/IEC 14496-2 (MPEG-4 Part 2), which interpolates bilinearly at up to 1/16 sample | 1999 | The chroma vector is the luma vector halved and kept at 1/8-sample precision (H.263 halves it too but rounds to half samples). Rounding as in MPEG-4's warping with rounding_control 0. Samples outside the frame repeat the edge. |
| Motion vector prediction | ITU-T H.263 section 6.1.1 and Annex F (four vectors per macroblock); Annex O for B-pictures | 1996, 1998 | As published: the median of three candidates (left, above, above-right, with Annex F's positions inside a macroblock), zero at the left edge, MV1 at the top edge, zero at the right edge; intra and skipped blocks count as zero; in B-frames, forward vectors predict from forward vectors and backward from backward, and a block without a vector in that direction counts as zero. A 16x16 macroblock with one vector uses its top-left block's predictor, as in Annex F. |
| Intra coefficient prediction | ITU-T H.263 Annex I, "Advanced INTRA coding mode" (the same family is in MPEG-4 Part 2, 1999) | 1998 | Three modes as published: DC from the mean of the left and above DC; DC and first coefficient row from above; DC and first column from the left. The mode is sent per 8x8 luma block, not per macroblock, and the zigzag scan is kept in all modes (Annex I switches to alternate scans). A neighbour coded in inter mode contributes the coefficients of its reconstruction instead of counting as unavailable. In DC mode with one neighbour outside the frame, the other is used alone; with none, mid-grey (1024). Chroma 4x4 intra blocks use the DC mode only, on samples level-shifted by 128 as in JPEG. |

## Transform, quantization and filtering

| Tool | Source | Date | Changes from the source |
|------|--------|------|-------------------------|
| Transform | N. Ahmed, T. Natarajan and K. R. Rao, "Discrete cosine transform", IEEE Trans. Computers C-23(1) | 1974 | Orthonormal DCT-II, 8x8 for luma and 4x4 for chroma, as a separable matrix product with the basis rounded to Q14 and each pass rounded to integers. |
| Scalar quantizer | G. J. Sullivan, "Efficient scalar quantization of exponential and Laplacian random variables", IEEE Trans. Information Theory 42(5) (uniform reconstruction with a dead zone) | 1996 | Levels are round((\|x\| - step/6) / step); reconstruction is level * step + step/6, away from zero. |
| Quantizer steps | ISO/IEC 13818-2 (MPEG-2 Video): a quantizer code indexes a non-linear table of step sizes (q_scale_type 1) | 1994 | Sinter's table has 64 entries, from 1 to 3072, growing roughly exponentially. The frame header carries the code; chroma adds an offset. |
| Coefficient scan | ITU-T T.81 (JPEG) zigzag scan | 1992 | As published, for 8x8 luma blocks. |
| In-loop deblocking filter | ITU-T H.263 Annex J, "Deblocking filter mode" (H.263 version 2) | 1998 | The published edge filter (d, UpDownRamp, d1, d2), edge rule (filtered unless both blocks are skipped) and order (horizontal edges, then vertical). The QUANT that indexes Table J.2 is a fifth of Sinter's quantizer step, rounded up, instead of half: Sinter's quantizer has a much narrower dead zone than H.263's, and half the step smoothed real detail (+6% by BD-rate). Table J.2 is continued past QUANT 31 as STRENGTH = (QUANT + 7) / 3. Chroma is filtered on its 4x4 transform-block edges. B-frames are filtered too. |

## Entropy coding

| Tool | Source | Date | Changes from the source |
|------|--------|------|-------------------------|
| Range coder | G. N. N. Martin, "Range encoding: an algorithm for removing redundancy from a digitised message", Video & Data Recording Conference, Southampton | 1979 | Bytes leave the top of a 32-bit range. A carry is resolved by holding back bytes that it could still change (the byte analogue of Witten, Neal & Cleary's bits_to_follow). The final value is chosen to end in zero bytes, which are not written. |
| Adaptive symbol models | I. H. Witten, R. M. Neal and J. G. Cleary, "Arithmetic coding for data compression", Communications of the ACM 30(6) | 1987 | A count per symbol, incremented on use, with all counts halved when their total passes a limit, as published. The starting count, increment and limit are set per alphabet (the paper uses 1, 1 and 2^14 - 1): 1, 128 and 65000 for coefficient-like alphabets, and 96, 96 and 8192 for mode flags. Models carry over from frame to frame of the same type and restart at key frames. |
| Trained starting tables | ITU-T H.263 Annex E, "Syntax-based arithmetic coding mode": arithmetic coding with fixed frequency tables for each syntax element | 1996 | The tables set where each adaptive model starts (Witten, Neal & Cleary's counts) instead of fixing it for good: per model, per model set (key and P-frames, B-frames) and per QP class (QP 0-11, 12-19, 20-27, 28+) of the frame where the set restarts. Probabilities in Q15, scaled at restart to 64 observations for coefficient-like alphabets and 16 for mode flags. Trained on ten Xiph CIF clips not used for evaluation (`tools/train/`). |
| Context modeling | J. Rissanen and G. G. Langdon, "Universal modeling and coding", IEEE Trans. Information Theory 27(1) | 1981 | Each symbol is coded with an adaptive model chosen by information already decoded: block type, band, luma or chroma, frame type, previous band zero or not. |
| Coded-band flags | The coded block pattern of ITU-T H.261, which flags the blocks of a macroblock that have nonzero coefficients; here applied to bands of the zigzag scan | 1988 | Each luma block's 63 AC coefficients, in zigzag order, form 16 bands. Each band has a flag: all zero, or its levels follow. |
| Luma AC levels as binary decisions | ITU-T T.81 (JPEG) arithmetic coding of AC coefficients, Annex F.1.4.4.2: a zero decision, a sign decision, a "magnitude above one" decision, the magnitude category in unary, then the magnitude bits, each with its own adaptive statistics | 1992 | Zero and "above one" have a model per block type and zigzag index; the category decisions per block type, low or high index (above 5) and step; the magnitude bits per block type and category. A band's coded flag replaces JPEG's end-of-block decision, and when every earlier level of a coded band is zero the last is known to be nonzero, so its zero decision is skipped. The models are Witten, Neal & Cleary's counts. |
| Contexts from the prediction | New (docs/NOVEL.md, section 2; search record there) | 2026 | The DCT of an inter block's motion-compensated prediction, compared with the quantizer step, selects the models for the block's band flags and its levels' zero and "above one" decisions. |
| Prediction agreement for mode flags | New (docs/NOVEL.md, section 4; search record there) | 2026 | Two predictions the decoder can form before reading a block's mode (P: skip motion and median predictor; B: forward and backward predictors) are compared; their mean absolute difference against the quantizer step selects the models for the macroblock mode and the inter and skip flags. |
| Large values | J. Teuhola, "A compression method for clustered bit-vectors", Information Processing Letters 7(6) (exponential Golomb codes) | 1978 | DC levels and vector differences are mapped to unsigned numbers by interleaving signs. Values 0..254 are range-coded symbols; 255 is an escape, and the value follows as an order-0 exponential Golomb code in a separate raw bit section of the frame. |

## Removed

- rANS entropy coding (`libsinter/core/entropy/rans.c`, no longer built), replaced by the range coder.
- The 6-tap Lanczos luma filter, whose half-sample taps were within one step of H.264's, replaced by the B-spline.
- Skip at the median-predicted vector (like H.264's P_Skip), replaced by H.263's zero-motion skip.
- Pixel-domain vertical, horizontal and average intra predictors (like H.264's), and Sinter's own low-AC prediction, replaced by H.263 Annex I.
- The H.264-style deblocking filter (alpha/beta activity thresholds, tc clip), replaced by H.263 Annex J.
- PVQ: its reconstruction used Daala's greedy energy correction, and it no longer paid for itself.
- Film grain synthesis: an AR model like AV1's, which the encoder never used.
- Floating-point CSF band weights in the decoder: they only set PVQ's pulse count.
