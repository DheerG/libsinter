# Provenance of Sinter's format tools

A format tool is anything the decoder must run to reproduce the output: syntax, entropy coding, prediction, transforms, filters and reconstruction. Each one must either come from a publication dated before 2004, implemented as published with small listed changes, or be new to Sinter, with a record of the search for the same idea in patents and papers. Nothing comes from H.264/AVC, its drafts, or any later standard or codec (VP8 onward, Daala, AV1).

Encoder-only methods (motion search, mode decisions, rate control, quantizer choices) don't affect the format and aren't listed.

This file records where each tool comes from. It is not a patent clearance.

| Tool | Source | Date | Changes from the source |
|------|--------|------|-------------------------|
| Range coder | G. N. N. Martin, "Range encoding: an algorithm for removing redundancy from a digitised message", Video & Data Recording Conference, Southampton | 1979 | Bytes leave the top of a 32-bit range. A carry is resolved by holding back bytes that it could still change (the byte analogue of Witten, Neal & Cleary's bits_to_follow). The final value is chosen to end in zero bytes, which are not written. |
| Adaptive symbol models | I. H. Witten, R. M. Neal and J. G. Cleary, "Arithmetic coding for data compression", Communications of the ACM 30(6) | 1987 | A count per symbol, incremented on use, with all counts halved when their total passes a limit, as published. The starting count, increment and limit are set per alphabet (the paper uses 1, 1 and 2^14 - 1): 1, 128 and 65000 for coefficient-like alphabets, and 96, 96 and 8192 for mode flags. |
| Luma interpolation (quarter-pel motion compensation) | M. Unser, A. Aldroubi and M. Eden, "Fast B-spline transforms for continuous image representation and interpolation", IEEE Trans. PAMI 13(3); and "B-spline signal processing, Part II", IEEE Trans. Signal Processing 41(2) | 1991, 1993 | Cubic B-spline with the published recursive prefilter (pole sqrt(3) - 2, mirror boundaries), in fixed point: constants in Q16, coefficients in Q8. The frame is first extended by repeating its edge samples 32 pixels on each side. Whole-sample positions copy the reference, which equals the exact spline there. |
| Skipped blocks | ITU-T H.263, "Video coding for low bit rate communication" (COD = 1: a macroblock not coded, copied from the reference at zero motion) | 1996 | Applies to 8x8 blocks and 16x16 macroblocks. In B-frames a skipped block is the rounded average of both references at zero motion. A skipped block counts as zero motion for its neighbours' motion vector prediction, as in H.263. |
| Intra coefficient prediction | ITU-T H.263 Annex I, "Advanced INTRA coding mode" (also in MPEG-4 Part 2, 1999) | 1998 | Three modes as published: DC from the mean of the left and above DC; DC and first coefficient row from above; DC and first column from the left. The mode is sent per 8x8 luma block, not per macroblock, and the zigzag scan is kept in all modes (Annex I switches to alternate scans). A neighbour coded in inter mode contributes the coefficients of its reconstruction instead of counting as unavailable. In DC mode with one neighbour outside the frame, the other is used alone; with none, mid-grey (1024). |
| In-loop deblocking filter | ITU-T H.263 Annex J, "Deblocking filter mode" (H.263 version 2) | 1998 | The published edge filter (d, UpDownRamp, d1, d2), edge rule (filtered unless both blocks are skipped) and order (horizontal edges, then vertical). The QUANT that indexes Table J.2 is a fifth of Sinter's quantizer step, rounded up, instead of half: Sinter's quantizer has a much narrower dead zone than H.263's, and half the step smoothed real detail (+6% by BD-rate). Table J.2 is continued past QUANT 31 as STRENGTH = (QUANT + 7) / 3. Chroma is filtered on its 4x4 transform-block edges. B-frames are filtered too. |
| Coded-band flags | The coded block pattern of ITU-T H.261 (1988), which flags the blocks of a macroblock that have nonzero coefficients; here applied to bands of the zigzag scan | 1988 | Each luma block's 63 AC coefficients, in zigzag order, form 16 bands. Each band has a flag: all zero, or its levels follow. The flag's model is chosen by block type, band and whether the previous band was nonzero (context modeling, below). |
| Context modeling | J. Rissanen and G. G. Langdon, "Universal modeling and coding", IEEE Trans. Information Theory 27(1) | 1981 | Each symbol is coded with an adaptive model chosen by information already decoded: block type, band, luma or chroma, frame type, previous band zero or not. |
| Motion vector prediction | ITU-T H.263 section 6.1.1 and Annex F (four vectors per macroblock); Annex O for B-pictures | 1996, 1998 | As published: the median of three candidates (left, above, above-right, with Annex F's positions inside a macroblock), zero at the left edge, MV1 at the top edge, zero at the right edge; intra and skipped blocks count as zero; in B-frames, forward vectors predict from forward vectors and backward from backward, and a block without a vector in that direction counts as zero. Every 8x8 block has its own vector, and a 16x16 macroblock with one vector uses its top-left block's predictor, as in Annex F. |

`libsinter/core/entropy/rans.c` is no longer part of the format or the build.

Removed from the format: PVQ (its reconstruction used Daala's greedy energy correction, and it no longer paid for itself), film grain synthesis (an AR model like AV1's; the encoder never used it), the pixel-domain intra predictors and the H.264-style deblocking filter.
